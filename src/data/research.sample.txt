SAMPLE RESEARCH NOTES (not the original research document)

This is a small, generic placeholder standing in for a longer private research
document. It exists so the Daily Coach feature has some real, public,
evidence-based text to ground its advice in when no private research file is
present. Drop your own longer file at src/data/research.private.txt
(gitignored) to use real content instead.

Spaced repetition and the forgetting curve: Hermann Ebbinghaus's 1885 work
showed that newly learned information decays roughly exponentially without
review, with much of it lost within the first day. Each successful review
flattens that curve further out, which is the basic mechanism behind spaced
repetition schedulers (including FSRS, used in this app): reviews are timed
to land just before a card is likely to be forgotten, not on a fixed
schedule.

Comprehensible input: Stephen Krashen's input hypothesis argues that language
acquisition happens most efficiently from input that is understandable but
slightly above the learner's current level ("i+1"), rather than from
explicit grammar instruction alone. This motivates reading/listening
practice pitched just above current vocabulary level rather than material
that is either trivially easy or overwhelmingly hard.

Output and production: Merrill Swain's output hypothesis suggests that
producing language (speaking or writing), not just receiving it, forces
learners to notice gaps in their own knowledge and pushes vocabulary from
passive recognition toward active recall — part of the motivation for
dedicated speaking/writing practice alongside reading and flashcards.

Vocabulary coverage: research on lexical coverage (e.g. Nation's work on
vocabulary size and text coverage) suggests comprehension of everyday text
improves sharply as known-word coverage approaches the high-90s percent
range, which is part of the motivation for frequency-ranked vocabulary study
— learning the most common words first covers the most ground per word
learned.
